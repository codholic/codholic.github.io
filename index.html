Publications

A Novel Low Power Single Bit SRAM Cell Using Quasi-Adiabatic Logic

Mohammad Redwan Islam, Susmita Karmaker, Md. Abrar Ibtesham, Irfan Rahman

IEEE ICECS 2022 · 29th IEEE Int. Conf. on Electronics, Circuits and Systems
Paper Slides Talk video
Abstract

SRAM draws a significant share of system power, so reducing its consumption lowers overall power. Adiabatic logic, which slows charging and discharging to reduce energy dissipation, is a promising technique for ultra-low-power design where power matters more than speed. This paper proposes an SRAM cell using a novel quasi-adiabatic logic that draws less power than its predecessors. Simulations were performed in Cadence Virtuoso using gpdk090.

Modified ECRL Adiabatic Logic for Ultra Low Power VLSI Applications

Mohammad Redwan Islam, Takibul Islam Sabbi, Nafiul Islam Ananta, Saroar Jaman Badhon, Satyendra N. Biswas

ICEEICT 2021 · 5th Int. Conf. on Electrical Engineering and Information Communication Technology
Paper Slides Talk video
Abstract

Efficient Charge Recovery Logic (ECRL) is one of the widely accepted adiabatic design techniques. This work proposes a modified ECRL circuit with lower power and energy dissipation. Simulations in LTspice XVII with the 16 nm PTM LP model (NIMO Group, Arizona State University) show that the proposed topology outperforms conventional ECRL.

Unsupervised Abnormality Detection Using Heterogenous Autonomous System

Kazi Mejbaul Islam, Rouhan Noor, Sayeed Shafayet Chowdhury, Tafannum Tahiat Ohi, Mohammad Redwan Islam, et al.

IEEE TENCON 2020 · IEEE Region 10 Conference
Paper
Abstract

A heterogeneous system that estimates the degree of anomaly in an unmanned surveillance drone from IMU sensor data and real-time images, in an unsupervised way. AngleNet detects images taken in an abnormal state and is ensembled with an autoencoder fed by IMU data. Based on our IEEE SP Cup 2020 entry, the method achieved 97.3% accuracy on the provided dataset and was further validated on an in-house setup.

Undergraduate Thesis

Design and Performance Analysis of a Novel Low Power SRAM Cell with Stable Write and Readability Using Quasi-Adiabatic Logic

AUST, 2021 · Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Satyendra N. Biswas

Thesis Slides
Summary

As technology scales into the nanometer regime, leakage becomes a dominant part of total power. This thesis explores adiabatic design topologies, implements and compares logic circuits built with them, and designs an SRAM cell using adiabatic logic.
